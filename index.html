Intro

I currently work at Facebook AI Applied Research, where I solve AI problems that lie at the intersection of research and product. Previously, I got my bachelors in computer science from the University of Washington, where I did nearly 2 years of undergraduate research in Natural Language Processing (NLP). My undergrad advisor was Luke Zettlemoyer. Before UW, I went to “high school” through the Robinson Center’s transition school to early entrance program.

Within computer science my main interest is natural language processing, a subset of machine learning that focuses on language. My fascination with language originally stemmed from a book I read in Transition School English--Catherine Belsey’s Critical Practice. It’s a fairly dense English/linguistic philosophy textbook that goes into depth on post-structural and post-Sassurean theory (think Barthes, Derrida, possibly even Foucault). I won’t claim to have understood it 100%, but regardless, the book changed my entire outlook and understanding of language. It led to my current adamant belief that language is absolutely foundational to our society--a la Catherine Belsey, language defines our social ideologies, is defined /by/ our social ideologies, and the meaning derived from text is moreso a reflection of the social structures at the time and place of its composition than it is a reflection of writer or reader intent.

Thus, I believe so-called “teaching machines to read and write” is probably one of the most intriguing, difficult, and potentially problematic (if we are not careful) challenges of the 21st century. I also believe will be the main driving force towards artificial general intelligence (if we will ever able to achieve that).

But enough abstract futuristic talk, some specific problems I’m interested in include:

  • Generalizability: can we make NLP models extrapolate to arbitrary, possibly unseen, tasks?
  • Interpretability: current deep learning models, esp. state-of-the-art ones in NLP (i.e. BERT) are often described as “black boxes.” Can we make them more transparent? And more importantly, without sacrificing accuracy? I believe interpretability will be key in driving our field forward.
  • Scalability and resource efficiency: current models are extremely costly to train, and have significant downstream environmental and economic impacts. How can we mitigate these issue?
  • AI for social good: reducing social bias in NLP models (see above paragraphs), misinformation or fake news detection, etc.
This list is by no means comprehensive--just a taste of the interesting work I’ve been exposed to during my undergrad and time at Facebook.
